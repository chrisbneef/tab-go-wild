### Post 2 — Moss myth
Please don't pressure wash your roof. 🙅
Oregon State University Extension warns it can damage shingles. High pressure strips the granules that protect asphalt shingles and can push water underneath.
Better: a long-handled scrub brush (or a pro), a treatment applied per the label, and trimming branches so more sun hits the roof. OSU says more sunlight is the most effective long-term fix.

### Post 4 — CCB license check
In Oregon, every contractor who advertises has to hold a Construction Contractors Board license, and show the number on their website.
Before anyone climbs on your roof:
✅ Get their CCB #
✅ Look it up at search.ccb.state.or.us
✅ Check it's active, the name matches, and look at complaint history
Takes 2 minutes. Saves a lot of grief.

### Post 6 — West Linn
West Linn homeowners: if your house went up in the 80s or 90s, your roof may be on its original shingles. The city's median home was built in 1988 (Census), and a 25–30-year asphalt roof from then is at the end of its life.
Signs to look for: curling shingles, granules in the gutters, stains on upstairs ceilings.
West Linn roofing guide (permits, CC&Rs, tree rules) 👉 willamettefallsroofing.com/areas/west-linn/

### Post 7 — Zinc strips
Do zinc strips stop roof moss? 🤔
OSU's pest guide says they're not effective at eliminating moss. Rain carries metal particles just below the strip, so moss stays off a narrow band, not the whole roof.
What works better: cleaning, treatment and more sunlight. Full breakdown 👉 willamettefallsroofing.com/services/moss-removal-roof-cleaning/

### Post 9 — Gutters
Under big firs, gutter guards help, but no guard is maintenance-free. Needles and moss sit on top.
Plan on at least one cleaning a year (late fall after the leaves drop), plus spring if you're under conifers. Overflowing gutters = wet fascia, stained siding and water at your foundation.
Gutter guide + costs 👉 willamettefallsroofing.com/services/gutters/

### Post 10 — Lake Oswego cedar
Lake Oswego has a lot of beautiful old cedar shake roofs. When it's time to replace one, the options are real cedar (most upkeep), synthetic shake (the look with less care), designer asphalt or standing-seam metal.
Heads-up: old cedar often sits on spaced "skip" sheathing, so switching materials usually means new solid decking. Make sure it's in the bid.
👉 willamettefallsroofing.com/areas/lake-oswego/

### Post 11 — Repair or replace
Repair or replace? A rough rule:
🔧 Repair: one leak, one spot, roof in the first two-thirds of its life
🏠 Replace: leaks in several places, curling across whole slopes, soft decking, or paying for repairs every year
Ask a licensed roofer to price both and compare over five years.
👉 willamettefallsroofing.com/services/roof-repair/

### Post 12 — Storm chasers
After a big storm, out-of-area crews go door to door. Oregon's CCB warns scam artists target storm victims.
🚩 No CCB number
🚩 "Sign today"
🚩 Big cash deposit
🚩 Asking you to hand over your insurance claim
Call your insurer first, check the license, get it in writing. Storm first-steps 👉 willamettefallsroofing.com/resources/storm-leak-checklist/

### Post 14 — Oregon City historic
Own a historic home in Oregon City's McLoughlin or Canemah neighborhoods? Exterior changes there can need design review, so check with the city before switching roof materials or colors.
And older brick chimneys are a top leak source: ask for new step and counter flashing, not just sealant.

### Post 15 — Permits
"Do I need a permit to reroof?" 📝
Oregon's Building Codes Division lists "applying roofing when all of the old roofing is removed, and all new roof sheathing is installed" as permit work. Keep your existing sheathing and you generally don't need one, but check your city. Building office numbers for all 5 south metro cities 👉 willamettefallsroofing.com/faq/
